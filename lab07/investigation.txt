Questions answers:
1. Which targeted usernames are current accounts?
The current accounts are alice, bob, eve and david. None of them had failed login attempts.
The attacker mainly targeted old accounts and common usernames like admin, root and backup. 
This suggests the attacker was guessing common system account names rather than targeting specific current users.

2. Which targeted names are legacy accounts? Which would be most dangerous?
The legacy accounts are admin, root and backup, with 17, 10 and 4 failed login attempts.
Root would be the most dangerous if it still existed and allowed login, because it could give an attacker full control of the system. 
Admin could also be dangerous if it had administrator privileges.

3. Were any accepted logins seen for disabled accounts or external addresses?
No accepted logins were found for the disabled account david. 
All the provided successful login records came from internal IP addresses (192.168.1.x), although the complete log still needs to be checked for external addresses. 
This is a good sign, but it does not prove the system is completely secure or that every login was authorised.

4. Which accounts had plaintext credentials and successful logins?
Alice and bob both had plaintext passwords stored on the workstation and successful logins. 
Alice had 6 successful logins from 4 different internal IP addresses, while bob had 5 successful logins from 3 different internal IP addresses.
This is worth reporting because exposed passwords could be used to access these accounts without permission.
However, the logs alone do not prove that the accounts were compromised.
